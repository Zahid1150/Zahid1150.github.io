Doctoral Research · Clemson University

Multi-Hospital Physician Scheduling

Lead doctoral researcher on a CDC/NIOSH-funded, multi-institution project with Prisma Health, working with faculty in industrial engineering, psychology, emergency medicine, and sleep science.

  • Turned clinician and hospital requirements into scheduling models (MILP and CP-SAT) covering staffing, eligibility, credentials, rest, fatigue, fairness, and preferences.
  • Implemented in Python with Gurobi and Google OR-Tools on a high-performance computing cluster.
  • Built a bi-objective model trading clinician fatigue against schedule quality and fairness over a 90-day horizon.
  • Combined schedules, surveys, interviews, and wearable-device data into model inputs and evaluation measures.

Result: schedules met an average of 84–87% of each physician's weighted preference categories across synthetic 25–75-physician benchmarks while satisfying all hard staffing rules.

Research program: clinician scheduling and well-being

Qualitative interview study

How shift schedules shape clinician fatigue

Semi-structured interviews with 13 physicians and 18 nurses at three Prisma Health emergency departments, coded with collaboratively developed codebooks. Both groups linked fatigue to unpredictable shifts, high workload, and insufficient recovery.

Poster, Prisma Health Research Showcase, Nov 2025 · manuscript in preparation
Poster ↗
Multi-site survey study

Preferences, schedule alignment, and burnout

Survey of 103 nurses and 57 attending physicians at seven emergency departments. Nurses most often preferred 12-hour shifts and physicians 9-hour shifts; better alignment between schedules and preferences was associated with lower burnout and fatigue.

Poster, Prisma Health Research Showcase, Oct 2026 · manuscript in preparation

Optimization

Fatigue-aware multi-site physician rostering

Mixed-integer model for physicians rotating across a multi-hospital system that combines coverage rules with protected recovery after nights, fair sharing of night, weekend, and distant shifts, and a shift fatigue index built from chronotype, shift end time, and shift length.

Presented at IISE Annual Conference (May 2026) and HSPI (Feb 2026)
Optimization

Physician scheduling with individual preferences

With Dr. Kevin Taaffe and Dr. Hervé Kerivin: a mixed-integer model in which each physician distributes 100 points across preference categories (nights, weekends, protected days, work blocks, shift types), turned into physician-specific objective weights.

Accepted talk, INFORMS Annual Meeting, Nov 2026

LLMs for thematic analysis
